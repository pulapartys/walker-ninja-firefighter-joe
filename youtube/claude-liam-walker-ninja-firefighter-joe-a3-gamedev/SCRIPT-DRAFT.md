Extinguisho is sixty-four pixels tall; she fixed him at one point eight metres, about two point eight centimetres per pixel. Each prop got dimensions in metres, a bottom-centre origin, flat colours and a triangle budget under five hundred, plus four predicted failures, each with a check. |
| B04 | Learning the window | her screenshot `blender-session.png` | First she explored Blender by hand. Blender is Z-up; Godot is Y-up. And the default cube's origin sits at its centre, so its base is one metre below the grid. That observation became prediction two: a prop that sinks into the floor. |
| B05 | Connecting the M C P | her MCP panel screenshot + the setup error | The setup line in the assignment failed: Claude Code's dash e option accepts several values, and swallowed the server name. | B06 | A prompt and what it built | hydrant plan → render → her viewport check | One asset per request, plan first. For the hydrant Claude proposed six primitives with reduced segment counts: three hundred fifty-six triangles, against a default sphere's nine hundred sixty. After her approval, each part was one execute blender code call, then a join, an origin set to the world origin, and transform apply, leaving scale at one. |
| B07 | Barrel to backpack | bag v1 → v2 → v3 → v4 | The bag shows the limit of numbers. Version one matched its brief dimensions within two centimetres, yet read as a barrel, with the handle torus buried inside the body. Sreeja asked what the bag is for: it rides on his back. Three revisions later it is a rounded backpack, two hundred ninety-six triangles. |
| B08 | Ledge to flame | ledge → flame v1, v6, v8 | Her third prop began as a ledge derived from the level data, ninety by sixteen pixels. It matched the brief, and was, in her words, nothing interesting, so she swapped it for a flame. Built from stacked primitives, every flame read as a Christmas tree, crystals or spikes. |
| B09 | One flame first | her brief → grey single flame → coloured | So she changed the method. One mesh, built in bmesh from hexagonal cross-sections with independent left and right widths, approved in grey before colour. Faces were coloured by height and by normal direction, brighter in the centre. Forty-eight triangles. |
| B10 | Her hand edit | her three hand-edit screenshots | Then her own edit, in the viewport. Edit mode, vertex select, the tip vertex, G then X: a constrained move along global X of nought point one nine metres. On screen the drag ran toward the centre, because an axis's screen direction depends on the view. She kept the straighter flame. |
| B11 | Three props in one scene | code: `props_room.tscn` lines 48–55 | Export used the glTF exporter with plus Y up and transforms applied. In the scene file each prop is an instanced dot G L B with an identity basis; only the origin column moves. No rotation, no scale. A one point eight metre cylinder stands in for Extinguisho. |
| B12 | In the engine | **real footage:** Godot Movie Maker orbit | This is the running engine, recorded by Movie Maker at a fixed thirty frames per second. Upright, grounded, at game scale beside the pole. Blender's Z-up became Godot's Y-up, so prediction one did not happen. |
| B13 | The headless check | code: `check_props.gd` lines 51–58 | The headless check pushes every mesh's bounding box through its global transform, so a rotated or scaled node can't hide behind a correct mesh. Then it tests size within a centimetre of the brief, every scale at one, height along Y, the bottom against the floor surface, and the triangle count. |
| B14 | The numbers, labelled headless | recorded check output + planted-fault run | Headless, all three pass, matching the brief to the millimetre. Mutation mode plants three faults: a hydrant rotated ninety degrees, a bag scaled two times, a flame lowered twenty centimetres. All three fail. And one trap: Godot exited with code zero when the script failed to parse, so the runner now requires a printed pass. |
| B15 | What the check can't see | her Godot screenshot + her words | The check proves geometry, not appearance. | B16 | Verdict | verdict card | The verdict. Verified in Blender and headless Godot: dimensions, unit scale, Y-up orientation, grounded origins and triangle budgets. Sreeja directed, decided and reviewed; Claude Code proposed and implemented. Source revision on screen. |
